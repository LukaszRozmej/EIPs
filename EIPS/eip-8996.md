## Abstract

This proposal applies the milli-gas counter of [EIP-8053](./eip-8053.md) to the execution-gas dimension of [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md). Execution-gas is metered in milli-gas, one thousandth of a gas. State-gas, the state-gas reservoir and the refund counter stay in gas units. Contracts see gas units: `GAS`, the gas argument of calls and the call stipend do not change their meaning. The proposal also sets a rule for the cost schedule: an execution-gas cost can go down to a fraction of a gas, but it cannot go up. State creation is priced only through `CPSB` in the state-gas dimension, which limits state growth whatever execution-gas costs.

## Motivation

[EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md) prices new state per byte in a separate dimension. This limits state growth at a higher gas limit. [EIP-8053](./eip-8053.md) allows costs that are a fraction of a gas, so cheap operations can be priced near their execution time. The two proposals solve different problems:

- **Milli-gas alone does not limit state growth.** At a 60M gas limit and the prices before [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md), a block can spend half of its gas on code deposit at 200 gas per byte. This is 367 GiB of new state per year, against ~116 GiB per year observed in January 2026. When execution costs go down, each transaction uses less gas, and more transactions fit in a block. Each of these transactions creates as much state as before, so the share of block gas spent on state creation increases. If `f` is the share of gas that compute uses and compute becomes `k` times cheaper, observed state growth increases by a factor of `1 / ((1 - f) + f / k)`.
- **Cost increases break deployed contracts.** A contract that forwards a fixed gas amount, relies on the 2,300-gas stipend or compares `gasleft()` with a constant can fail after an execution cost goes up. A cost decrease cannot cause such a failure: every gas budget that was sufficient stays sufficient.

With both proposals, the state-gas dimension of [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md) limits state growth, and milli-gas lets execution-gas costs go down, which deployed contracts tolerate. This proposal specifies how the two counters work together, and the rule that execution-gas costs only go down.

## Specification

The terms `gas_left`, `state_gas_reservoir`, `evm_state_gas_used`, legacy share, refill and snapshot have the meaning that [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md) gives them. `state_gas_ceiling` has the meaning that [EIP-8998](./eip-8998.md) gives it.

### Parameters

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| `MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS` | 1000 |

### Units of the gas counters

The `gas_left` counter of [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md) is replaced by `milli_gas_left`, which counts milli-gas. All other counters keep gas units:

| Counter | Unit |
|---|---|
| `milli_gas_left` | milli-gas |
| `state_gas_reservoir`, `evm_state_gas_used`, `state_gas_used_snapshot`, `state_gas_reservoir_snapshot` | gas |
| `state_gas_ceiling` ([EIP-8998](./eip-8998.md)) | gas |
| `refund_counter` | gas |
| `tx.gas`, `intrinsic_gas`, `calldata_floor_gas_cost`, block counters, header `gas_used` | gas |

A charge of `c` gas against `milli_gas_left` deducts `c * MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS`. A comparison of `milli_gas_left` with an amount in gas multiplies that amount by `MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS`.

### Start of the transaction

The split of [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md) is computed in gas units, and `gas_left` is then converted:

```python
evm_gas = tx.gas - intrinsic_gas
execution_gas_budget = TX_MAX_GAS_LIMIT - intrinsic_gas
gas_left = min(execution_gas_budget, evm_gas)
milli_gas_left = gas_left * MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS
state_gas_reservoir = evm_gas - gas_left
```

The charges of the pre-execution phase of [EIP-2780](./eip-2780.md) are amounts in gas. Their execution-gas parts, legacy shares and the parts that the reservoir cannot pay are deducted from `milli_gas_left` as described above. If the transaction fails before the top-level frame starts, `milli_gas_left` is set to 0, as [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md) sets `gas_left` to 0.

### Execution-gas charges

Each execution-gas cost has a value in milli-gas. The value is `MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS` times the cost in gas before this proposal, unless the cost schedule of this proposal lowers it (see [Cost schedule](#cost-schedule)). A cost that a formula computes, such as memory expansion, copy costs, `KECCAK256` and precompile costs, is computed in gas units as before and multiplied by `MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS`, unless the cost schedule gives a formula in milli-gas.

If `milli_gas_left` is less than a charge, the frame halts exceptionally, as on out-of-gas.

### State-gas charges

The amount of each state-gas charge stays as [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md) specifies, in gas. The charge is paid in the order of [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md), with the parts that `gas_left` pays converted to milli-gas:

```python
def charge_state_gas(evm, amount, legacy_share):
    if evm.milli_gas_left < legacy_share * MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS:
        raise OutOfGasError
    evm.milli_gas_left -= legacy_share * MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS

    rest = amount - legacy_share
    from_reservoir = min(rest, evm.state_gas_reservoir)
    evm.state_gas_reservoir -= from_reservoir
    rest -= from_reservoir

    if evm.milli_gas_left < rest * MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS:
        raise OutOfGasError
    evm.milli_gas_left -= rest * MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS

    evm.evm_state_gas_used += amount
    if evm.evm_state_gas_used > evm.state_gas_ceiling:  # EIP-8998
        raise OutOfGasError
```

Refills, reverts and exceptional halts follow [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md) without change. A refill and the credit of a revert go to `state_gas_reservoir`, in gas. They never credit `milli_gas_left`. An exceptional halt consumes the frame's `milli_gas_left`.

### Gas that contracts observe

The rules of [EIP-8053](./eip-8053.md) for the contract boundary apply:

- `GAS` pushes `milli_gas_left // MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS`. It does not include the reservoir, as in [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md).
- The gas argument of `CALL`, `CALLCODE`, `DELEGATECALL` and `STATICCALL` is in gas units. The part that the [EIP-150](./eip-150.md) rule keeps in the calling frame is computed in whole gas, as in [EIP-8053](./eip-8053.md). `CREATE` and `CREATE2` forward the available milli-gas less this part.
- The call stipend adds `GAS_CALL_STIPEND * MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS` to the child frame.
- A child frame returns its unused `milli_gas_left` to its parent exactly.
- The `SSTORE` stipend check of [EIP-2200](./eip-2200.md) compares `milli_gas_left` with `GAS_CALL_STIPEND * MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS`. As in [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md), the reservoir is not included.

A child frame can use the whole reservoir, as in [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md). An instruction that pushes the reservoir pushes it in gas units, without conversion.

### State-gas allowance of call frames

[EIP-8998](./eip-8998.md) computes the allowance of a child frame from the gas that the child frame starts with. With milli-gas, the allowance is computed from the exact milli-gas of the child frame:

```python
child.state_gas_ceiling = min(
    parent.state_gas_ceiling,  # no limit if the parent has none
    evm_state_gas_used
    + child_milli_gas * CPSB // (LEGACY_GAS_PER_STATE_BYTE * MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS))
```

`child_milli_gas` is the milli-gas that the child frame starts with, after the [EIP-150](./eip-150.md) rule and including the call stipend. When `child_milli_gas` is a multiple of `MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS`, the result is the same as in [EIP-8998](./eip-8998.md).

### End of the transaction

At the end of the transaction, the gas left is rounded down:

```python
tx_output.gas_left = milli_gas_left // MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS
```

All other end-of-transaction rules of [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md) apply in gas units without change, including `tx_gas_used_before_refund`, the refund cap, the calldata floor, the block counters, the header `gas_used` and the base fee. `evm_state_gas_used` is an exact amount of gas, so the rounding goes only to the execution-gas dimension. A transaction pays less than one gas for rounding.

### System calls

A system call starts with `milli_gas_left = 30,000,000 * MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS` and the `state_gas_reservoir` of [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md), in gas.

### Cost schedule

The cost schedule divides the execution-gas costs into two groups:

| Group | Costs | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Compute | Arithmetic, comparison and bitwise operations; stack operations; `JUMP`, `JUMPI`, `JUMPDEST`, `PC`; memory operations and memory expansion; `KECCAK256`; copy costs per word; reads of the execution environment that do not access state; `TLOAD`, `TSTORE`; precompiles; the hash cost of the code deposit | Can go down |
| Access and write | The access and write costs of [EIP-8038](./eip-8038.md); the base and value costs of calls; `LOG*`; `CREATE_ACCESS`; `SELFDESTRUCT`; `BLOCKHASH` | Do not change |

The milli-gas cost of a compute operation `op` is:

```python
cost(op) = min(
    MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS * amsterdam_cost(op),
    ceil(MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS * TARGET_GAS_PER_SECOND * execution_time(op)))
```

- `amsterdam_cost(op)` is the cost in gas before this proposal.
- `execution_time(op)` is the measured time, in seconds, of `op` on the slowest client that is in common use, as in [EIP-7904](./eip-7904.md).
- `TARGET_GAS_PER_SECOND` is the gas per second that a block of compute operations must reach.

The values of `TARGET_GAS_PER_SECOND` and of `execution_time` are not yet given. They come from new measurements of the clients that implement [EIP-7928](./eip-7928.md).

Intrinsic gas, calldata costs and the calldata floor do not change. State-gas does not change: new state is priced only through `CPSB`.

## Rationale

### State-gas stays in gas units

Every state-gas charge is a whole multiple of `CPSB`, so milli-gas gives it no precision. In gas units, the reservoir, `evm_state_gas_used`, the snapshots and the ceiling of [EIP-8998](./eip-8998.md) stay exact. A contract that reads the reservoir reads a value in the same unit as `GAS`. The only rounding is at the end of the transaction, and only the execution-gas dimension takes it.

### Execution-gas costs only go down

A cost increase that contracts see can break a fixed gas budget: a gas argument, the stipend, or a `gasleft()` threshold. [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md) needs the legacy shares and [EIP-8998](./eip-8998.md) needs the allowance to limit these breaks for state creation. A cost decrease cannot break a budget. If an operation cost `c` before and costs `c' <= c` now, every execution path that had enough gas still has enough gas.

The `min` in the cost formula makes this a rule of the schedule, not a property that each later change must check.

### Execution-gas decreases do not increase state growth beyond the limit

The header `gas_used` of [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md) is the larger of the two block counters, and the base fee follows it. State-gas per block is therefore limited by the block gas limit, and its average by the gas target, whatever execution-gas costs. When execution-gas costs go down, more transactions fit in a block and observed state growth increases. It can increase only up to the limit of the state-gas dimension: 48 GiB per year at a 60M gas limit and 120 GiB per year at 150M ([EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md)).

### Access costs do not change

The worst-case I/O of a block depends on the access costs and the gas limit. If access costs stay and the gas limit stays, worst-case I/O stays. Compute decreases increase compute throughput without a change to worst-case I/O or to the limit on state growth. Throughput for state access needs a higher gas limit, with access costs that keep worst-case I/O within the client budget, as in [EIP-8038](./eip-8038.md).

### Size of the decreases

[EIP-7904](./eip-7904.md) finds that every compute operation that it examines reaches at least 100 Mgas/s at its current cost on the slowest client. If a 60M block must execute in 2 seconds, `TARGET_GAS_PER_SECOND` is 30M, and compute operations can be about 3 times cheaper. Some operations cost less than one gas at that rate. This is why the schedule needs milli-gas.

### Alternative: a rebase of gas units

[EIP-8059](./eip-8059.md) multiplies all costs and limits by 1000 and then lowers some of them. Contracts then see the multiplied values, but the constants in deployed code stay as they are: the 2,300-gas stipend, the gas arguments of calls and the `gasleft()` thresholds become 1000 times too small. Milli-gas with gas units at the contract boundary gives the same precision and keeps these constants correct.

## Backwards Compatibility

This proposal changes the cost of transactions and requires a scheduled network upgrade.

If the cost schedule lowers no cost, every charge is a whole number of gas, and `milli_gas_left` is always a multiple of `MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS`. A transaction then has the same result and the same gas used as before this proposal.

When the schedule lowers compute costs, contracts can see this:

- A compute-heavy failure handler becomes cheaper, so a handler that ran out of gas before can complete.
- A loop that runs until `gasleft()` is low runs more iterations.
- A callee with only the stipend can run more compute. It still cannot write storage, and it cannot pay the legacy share of any state creation.

The allowance of [EIP-8998](./eip-8998.md) depends only on the forwarded gas. A cheaper execution does not increase the state that a callee can create.

Gas estimation, receipts and the JSON-RPC interface keep gas units. Tracers SHOULD report gas in gas units, and MAY add the milli-gas values.

## Test Cases

All values are in gas unless shown as milli-gas.

- **No decrease.** With every cost at `MILLI_GAS_PER_GAS` times its value before this proposal, every transaction has the same result and the same gas used as under [EIP-8037](./eip-8037.md) and [EIP-8998](./eip-8998.md).
- **Legacy share and spill.** A frame with `milli_gas_left = 50,000,500`, a reservoir of 50,000, and a new slot (97,920, legacy share 10,000): `milli_gas_left` pays 10,000,000, the reservoir pays 50,000, and `milli_gas_left` pays the remaining 37,920,000. After the charge, `milli_gas_left = 2,080,500`, the reservoir is 0, and `GAS` returns 2,080.
- **Legacy share check.** A frame with `milli_gas_left = 9,999,999` and a full reservoir halts on a new slot.
- **Stipend check.** `SSTORE` succeeds the stipend check with `milli_gas_left = 2,300,001` and fails it with `milli_gas_left = 2,300,000`.
- **Allowance.** A child frame that starts with 50,000,500 milli-gas gets an allowance of `50,000,500 * 1530 // 200,000 = 382,503`.
- **Whole-gas retention.** A parent has 100,500 milli-gas available for a call after the call's own costs, and forwards all of it. The parent keeps `(100 // 64) * 1,000 = 1,000` milli-gas, and the child starts with 99,500 milli-gas. `GAS` in the child returns 99, as before this proposal for a parent with 100 gas.
- **End of the transaction.** A transaction that ends with `milli_gas_left = 1,999` has `tx_output.gas_left = 1`.
- **Refill.** A frame clears a slot that it created. The reservoir gets 97,920 and `milli_gas_left` does not change.

## Security Considerations

The state-gas dimension limits state growth whatever the execution-gas costs. Without it, a decrease of compute costs would increase the share of block gas that can go to state creation.

The access costs do not change, so worst-case I/O at a given gas limit does not change.

A compute cost that is too low lets a block take longer than the client budget. The cost formula uses the slowest client in common use, and the `min` keeps every cost at or below its value before this proposal, so a measurement error can make a cost too low but never too high. The measurements must include the worst case of each operation, for example the largest operands of `DIV`, `MOD`, `MULMOD` and `EXP`, and the worst inputs of each precompile.

`GAS` rounds down, so a contract never sees more gas than it has. Two `GAS` reads around a cheap operation can return the same value. A contract that bills a sub-call by the difference of two `GAS` reads, and of two reservoir reads, gets the cost of the call with an error of less than one gas.

The rounding at the end of the transaction is in favor of the protocol, so a transaction cannot get fractional execution for free.
